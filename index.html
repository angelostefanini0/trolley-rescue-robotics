The Trolley Problem in Rescue Robotics

A robot is already saving one person. May it change its plan to save five?

Intro (about 1 minute). The classic trolley problem of Foot and Thomson becomes a design problem: an autonomous rescue robot is already carrying one person and discovers five more trapped in a fire. The question is not only what is right, but which rule we should program.

A rescue already under way

1 person · being rescued

An autonomous robot is already carrying one injured person. He will die if not brought immediately to safety.

5 people · in danger

It detects that five other people are trapped in a fire. They can still be saved but they need instant rescue operation.

May it change its plan and save the five at the cost of the first person’s life?

Trolley Problem · Rescue robotics

About 1 minute. Stress that the rescue has already begun: this is what separates the case from the classic trolley, where the bystander has not yet done anything for anyone.

Two contrast cases

If the robot continues the rescue: it saves the person it carries. The five will therefore die.

Case A

Doorless

In this case the robot is able to freely access the building without obstructions. The robot could leave the person behind and go back to save the five.  

Case B

Doorstop

In this case, the people are stuck behind a door the robot is not able to keep open. The robot then  carries the person into the building and uses them to hold the door open and evacuate the five.

In both cases, if it saves the five, the first person dies.

About 2 minutes. Same outcome in both cases: one dies, five live. Only the way the robot gets there differs. Click to reveal the vote, then ask for a show of hands for A and for B before introducing any theory. Note the numbers to come back to them at the end.

The Means Principle

Question: does the plan require using the person as a means?

Case A · DOORLESS

The robot only needs to stop carrying the person to be able to turn back. 

The person is NOT used as a mean

Case B · Doorstop

The plan works only if the person holds the door open.

The person is clearly a mean to the end

Verdict: doorless is a YES, doorstop is a NO

Trolley Problem · Rescue robotics

About 1 minute. Kantian root: the formula of humanity, never treat a person merely as a means. Test: if the person were not there, would the plan still work? In A yes, the robot would turn back anyway. In B no, without the person the door stays shut. So the person’s presence is essential to B’s success, not to A’s.

Thomson’s first solution

Question: does the means chosen to save the five already violate a right of the person?

Case A · Doorless

Leaving a dying person on the road withdraws help, but it violates none of their rights: no one has a right that a rescue be carried through to the end.

No right violated

Case B · Doorstop

Carrying the person into the fire, whether they are alive or already dying, is something done to their body: it violates their right to bodily integrity.

Right violated

Trolley Problem · Rescue robotics

Verdict: doorless is a YES, doorstop is a NO: abandoning the person violates no right, while carrying them into the fire violates their bodily integrity.

About 2 minutes. Thomson (1976, 1985): what matters is whether the means itself infringes someone’s rights. In Switch the threat already exists and is redirected. In B the robot does something to the person that infringes their rights. In A, leaving a dying person on the road violates no right, because there is no right that a rescue be completed. In B, taking the person into the fire violates their bodily integrity, whether they are alive or dying. Note: in 2008 (Turning the Trolley) Thomson revised her view and argued the bystander may not throw the switch.

Why the Early Morning principle does not apply

Idea: early in the morning, before anyone knows whether they will be the one or the five, everyone would agree to a rule that saves five.

Example · Rarely uses candles

Someone who seldom uses candles at home can already expect to be among the five in a future fire.

Example · Poor health

Someone in fragile health is more likely to be the single, injured person who can be abandoned.

Verdict: the principle is biased. There is no moment when everyone is equally unsure whether they belong to the one or to the five, so their agreement cannot be assumed.

Trolley Problem · Rescue robotics

About 1 minute. The Early Morning argument justifies saving the five by appeal to what everyone would agree to ex ante, before knowing their position. The objection: that state of equal ignorance never exists. People can already estimate their own risks from their habits and health, so the rule favours some groups and burdens others, and the consent it relies on is not universal.

Foot: Doing and Allowing

Question: does the robot cause the harm, or let it happen?

Case A · Doorless

It stops the rescue and lets the person’s condition run its course.

Allowing: permissible

Case B · Doorstop

The death of the person is not a direct consequence of the fire but it is caused by its own injuries

Allowing: permissible

Verdict: doorless is a YES, doorstop is a YES: in both cases the person dies of their own injuries, so the robot lets them die rather than actively contributing to their death.

Trolley Problem · Rescue robotics

About 2 minutes. Foot (1967): negative duties (not to harm) weigh more than positive duties (to help). Saving five versus saving one is a conflict of positive duties, so numbers can decide. In both A and B the person dies of their own injuries, not of the fire, so in both cases the robot allows the death rather than causing it. Open point for discussion: is withdrawing aid you are already giving really just allowing? Compare unplugging a life-support machine.
